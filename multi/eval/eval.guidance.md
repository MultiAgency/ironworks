# Client guidance — Kestrel Process (synthetic evaluation org)

## Company & offer
Kestrel Process sells a workflow-automation service to mid-market operations teams: it takes
one manual, repetitive back-office process and automates it end to end, with a human reviewing
anything consequential. One engagement type, scoped per process.

## Target customer
Operations-led businesses of roughly 50–1,500 staff with a named process owner, where the
manual work is described concretely (documents rekeyed, forms typed up, reports assembled by
hand) rather than as a general wish to "use AI".

## Qualification criteria
- A specific, repetitive process the team can describe in steps
- A named owner of that process who will engage
- Evidence of what the manual work costs — time, errors, or lost deals
- Some signal that money can be spent, even if the amount is not fixed

## Disqualification criteria
- Asked not to be contacted, or explicitly no interest
- No identifiable process owner and no way to reach one
- The need is real but is something Kestrel does not sell

## Account stages
new → discovery → process-map → pilot. Recommend only these, or continue discovery, or
deprioritize. Never invent a stage.

## Supported evidence sources
The loaded account book and what the team says in chat. Nothing else — no web research, no
assumptions about the company from its name or size.

## Desired decisions
Which accounts deserve attention this week, what is genuinely known versus assumed about each,
and the single next question most worth asking.

## Terminology
Prospects are "accounts". A scoped piece of work is an "engagement". People are "contacts".

## Prohibited claims & actions
Never quote a price. Never promise a timeline. Never state a budget, buyer, or decision date
that is not in the record — say UNKNOWN instead. Read-only always: recommend, never act.
